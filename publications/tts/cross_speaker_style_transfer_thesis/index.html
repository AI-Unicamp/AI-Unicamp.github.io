Expressive Speech Representation: Cross-Speaker Style Transfer in Text-to-Speech via Disentanglement

Author: Lucas H. Ueda  ·  Advisor: Paula D. P. Costa  ·  Universidade Estadual de Campinas (UNICAMP)

💻 Crab GitHub Code 💻 SelfTTS GitHub Code
Modern Text-to-Speech (TTS) systems generate highly intelligible audio but frequently fail to reproduce genuine emotional expressiveness. Building exhaustive emotional datasets for every target voice is highly impractical. Cross-speaker style transfer aims to solve this limitation by mapping the emotional prosody of a reference speaker onto a target neutral voice. However, this process persistently suffers from information leakage, where the reference speaker’s unique vocal timbre or phonetic content inadvertently bleeds into the extracted style representation. This entanglement severely degrades both the synthesized vocal identity and the overall audio quality.

To address these representational challenges, this thesis introduces SelfTTS, an end-to-end expressive synthesis framework built upon the VITS architecture. SelfTTS incorporates an explicit embedding disentanglement strategy that leverages a cosine-similarity-based Gradient Reversal Layer combined with a Multi Positive Contrastive Learning (MPCL) objective to effectively isolate emotional style from speaker identity. Furthermore, the framework introduces a self-augmentation mechanism utilizing its internal voice conversion pathway to generate synthetic training data. This strategy provides the acoustic model with diverse speaker identities expressing the exact same emotional content. To thoroughly validate the underlying contrastive representation learning approach, this research concurrently developed Crab, a bimodal Speech Emotion Recognition model driven by a novel Multi-Layer Contrastive Supervision technique.

Extensive evaluations demonstrate the superiority of the proposed methodologies. The Crab model achieved state-of-the-art performance on naturalistic emotion datasets by effectively balancing global accuracy with the reliable recognition of minority classes. In the synthesis domain, SelfTTS significantly outperformed baseline architectures in subjective human perceptual evaluations regarding both speaker similarity and emotional adherence while maintaining high naturalness. Objective representational analyses using Centered Kernel Alignment confirmed that the proposed disentanglement strategy successfully purifies the latent embedding space. Additionally, rigorous statistical testing established Emotion Embedding Cosine Similarity (EECS) as a highly reliable automatic proxy for human perceptual evaluation. Ultimately, this research provides a robust and reproducible framework for advancing expressive speech synthesis and cross-speaker style transfer for entirely neutral voices.
